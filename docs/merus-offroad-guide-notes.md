# MERUS Off-Road Trail Guide Notes

Reference notes distilled from the MERUS Adventure Park Offroad Vehicle Trail Guide information provided for the trip. Use this file as background when deciding what belongs in the concise visitor-facing trails page; it is not a substitute for the park's current rules or complete guide.

## Park Overview

MERUS Adventure Park is in the Texas Panhandle and offers off-road routes through canyon and rim country. The park guide emphasizes responsible recreation, staying on designated routes, and protecting the natural and historic landscape.

## Conduct and Stewardship

- Be courteous to other visitors and follow posted signs and park staff directions.
- Alcohol is not allowed on trails; the supplied guide also prohibits drug use in the park. Inappropriate or aggressive conduct may result in removal from the park.
- Do not approach or feed wildlife.
- Do not vandalize natural or built features. Do not collect plants, animals, rocks, timber, artifacts, or relics; leave historic items in place.
- Stay on designated trails and designated areas. The guide identifies the Playground between the Back 40 and Back 40 Short Cut as the only area designated for free wheeling.
- Open fires are not allowed. Propane use is allowed; report uncontrolled fires immediately.
- Prepare for quickly changing environmental and weather conditions and follow Leave No Trace / Tread Lightly principles.

## Vehicle and Driver Requirements

According to the supplied park-guide excerpt:

- Motorcycles, ATVs, and side-by-sides are not permitted.
- Vehicles must carry a functional fire extinguisher.
- Vehicles must have an operable GMRS radio; the front office may offer radios for rent.
- Drivers must carry proof of current liability insurance in the vehicle.
- Vehicles must have a muffled exhaust; straight pipes and open headers are not allowed.
- Maximum tire size is 43 inches.
- Buggies and custom-built full-size vehicles are allowed only when properly equipped, including a firewall, and insured.
- Vehicles must stay on mapped, designated off-road trails except in the designated Playground.

Confirm that these requirements remain current with MERUS before the visit. The campsite's East Rise (Area 300) access separately requires a 4WD vehicle and a 4WD vehicle park pass; verify which pass applies to the planned routes.

## Trail Rating System

The guide describes a system using color to indicate a recommended driver skill level and a number to indicate the difficulty of an individual trail. It is based on systems used by OnX Offroad, Red Rock 4-Wheelers in Moab, and other off-road parks. MERUS also provides vehicle-modification recommendations.

Trail difficulty can increase with weather, erosion, and changing obstacles. Longer-wheelbase vehicles, including Gladiators, Tacomas, and full-size pickups, may find tight terrain harder than the rating suggests. The supplied excerpt says full-size pickups are not recommended on Advanced, Highly Experienced, or Extreme trails. Drivers should judge their own and their vehicle's capability against current conditions and park guidance.

### Color and Skill Levels

| Color | Recommended skill level | Vehicle guidance | Ratings shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Green | Beginner | Fully stock 4x4; no modifications needed | 1-3 |
| Blue | Intermediate | Some stock 4x4 vehicles or slightly lifted vehicles with 32-inch or larger tires | 4-6 |
| Black, triangle marker | Advanced | A 2-inch lift with 35-inch or larger tires is recommended | 7 |
| Black, diamond marker | Highly Experienced | A 3-inch lift with 37-inch or larger tires; lockers recommended | 8-9 |
| Purple, double-diamond marker | Extreme | High-clearance lift, 40-inch or larger tires, and lockers; expect body damage | 10 |

### Numbered Trail Ratings

**1 — Easy.** Dirt or rocky road with gentle grades. Suitable for all 4WD and most 2WD vehicles. Roads are typically wide enough for two vehicles.

**2 — Easy.** Dirt or rocky road that may be unmaintained after rain or snow. May include shallow water crossings and obstacles under 12 inches, including small ledges. Roads are typically one to two vehicles wide. Suitable for high-clearance 2WD vehicles.

**3 — Easy.** Uneven, rutted dirt trail with possible loose rocks and sandy washes. Water crossings are less than one foot deep. Mud holes and obstacles up to 12 inches, including ledges and short, steep grades, may occur. Roads are typically one vehicle wide with places to pass. Adverse weather can make this an intermediate/moderate trail.

**4 — Intermediate.** Uneven, rutted dirt trail with loose rocks, sand, erosion, and washes. Potential water crossings up to one foot deep, mud holes, and obstacles up to 18 inches, including ledges and short, steep grades. Roads are typically one vehicle wide with places to pass.

**5 — Intermediate.** Uneven, rutted dirt trail with loose rocks, sand, erosion, and washes. Potential water crossings up to 18 inches deep, mud holes, and obstacles up to 2 feet, including ledges and short, steep grades.

**6 — Intermediate.** Very rocky, rutted, off-camber trail with erosion, loose rocks, sand, washes, and obstacles up to 3 feet tall, including ledges and short, steep grades. Deep water crossings with strong currents may occur. Adverse weather can make this an advanced trail.

The supplied material gives the color/skill categories for ratings 7-10 but does not include their individual trail descriptions. Use the complete, current MERUS guide for detailed definitions and route-specific requirements.

## Route Details From Trail PDFs

The details below were transcribed from the supplied trail PDFs. Extracted trail photos are stored in the `trails/` folder. The processed trail-description PDFs were removed from the workspace after their details and images were captured.

### MERUS 101

- **Rating:** Green 3 (Beginner)
- **Length and typical time:** 4.5 miles / 40 minutes
- **Route:** Runs most of the length of MERUS Adventure Park, beginning with the Drop-In descent into the canyon and crossing Salt Fork Creek several times. The route passes through canyon terrain with short ledges and steep, short climbs and drops.
- **Difficulty and caution:** The Drop-In is a steep 21-degree rock, sand, and gravel descent. The rest of the trail includes short, steep climbs and tight, rocky steps. Rain can make the trail slick and creek crossings wet or muddy.
- **Getting there:** Travel south along Park Road West to the left turn for MERUS 101.
- **Side trips:** Deep Creek, Northbound, Salt Fork Sections 1 and 2, Middle Earth, Huckleberry, Sagissor Blue and Black, 101 Loop Back, Carnage, and the Back 40.
- **Notable:** The Drop-In descent and canyon views.

### Northbound

- **Rating:** Green 3 (Beginner)
- **Length and typical time:** 1.5 miles / 30 minutes
- **Route:** Scenic route through creek beds alongside the canyon walls toward the back of the canyon. The route passes local flora including star sunflowers, Blackfoot daisies, tansy asters, buffalograss, sagebrush, yucca, prickly pear, mesquite, cottonwood, western soapberry, and hackberry.
- **Difficulty and caution:** Generally easy, requires 4-low, and includes creek crossings that are usually dry unless it is actively raining, plus a few rocky climbs and descents.
- **Getting there:** Enters from MERUS 101 near an open old gate; it can also be accessed from Salt Fork Section 1, Tin Tin, and Kate the Great.
- **Side trips:** Crosses Salt Fork Section 1 several times, allowing short creek-bed explorations.
- **Notable:** Meadows Campground at the end of the trail offers a shaded rest or camping area beneath large trees supported by a spring farther up the creek bed.

### Roundabout

- **Rating:** Green 3 (Beginner)
- **Length and typical time:** 0.5 mile / 10 minutes
- **Route:** Scenic, tree-lined connector from Northbound to Tin Tin, with a creek crossing and panoramic canyon views. The source guide describes it as beginner-friendly and suitable for a novice in a stock vehicle.
- **Difficulty and caution:** A few descents and a creek crossing. Long-wheelbase vehicles with poor departure angles may have difficulty. The Tin Tin entrance/exit is soft sand and can be tricky to climb when dry.
- **Getting there:** Accessed from Tin Tin or Northbound.
- **Side trips:** None.
- **Notable:** A good place for a short break.

### Spicy

- **Rating:** Green 3 (Beginner)
- **Length and typical time:** 1 mile / 30 minutes
- **Route:** Starts and finishes on MERUS 101. From MERUS 101 it climbs toward the Boulders hike trailhead; turning south from the Boulders, it follows arroyos along the park's eastern boundary with quick, sharp turns. The guide recommends running north to south because afternoon sunshine can be blinding.
- **Difficulty and caution:** Despite the Green rating, its many tight turns require attention from start to finish. The guide describes it as a good route for beginners to build comfort before harder Blue trails and as a shoulder workout.
- **Side trips:** The Boulders hike.
- **Notable:** The Boulders hike has views from the top.

### Deep Creek

- **Rating:** Blue 4 (Intermediate)
- **Length and typical time:** 1 mile / 20 minutes
- **Route:** Narrow, rocky trail through a canyon riverbed, with red sandstone layers and white Satin Spar Gypsum veins. Cryptobiotic living soil is present along the ground and rocks.
- **Difficulty and caution:** Relatively easy, with small to medium rocks, narrow passages, little off-camber terrain, and some sharp turns. Rocks are frequent; choose lines and place tires carefully to avoid high-centering or catching differentials and suspension components.
- **Getting there:** Enter from MERUS 101 and exit onto Salt Fork Section 2.
- **Side trips:** None.
- **Notable:** Useful trail for practicing tire placement.

### Salt Fork Section 2

- **Rating:** Blue 5 (Intermediate)
- **Length and typical time:** 0.38 mile / 15 minutes
- **Route:** Short creek-bed trail following a historic fork of Salt Fork Creek, which helped carve Palo Duro Canyon. It has medium-sized rocks and multiple possible lines, and leads to Lonesome Dove and Salt Fork Section 3.
- **Difficulty and caution:** Line options range from relatively easy to difficult. Small to large rocks, sand, and red clay make up the creek bed; tire placement matters because vehicles can get hung up. The guide warns the route can be dangerous in rain.
- **Getting there:** Accessible from MERUS 101, Deep Creek, Lonesome Dove, and Salt Fork Sections 1 and 3.
- **Side trips:** Entrances to Lonesome Dove and Salt Fork Section 3 are along the trail.
- **Notable:** A dry waterfall offers a dramatic photo backdrop.

### The Chute

- **Rating:** Blue 4 (Intermediate)
- **Length and typical time:** 1 mile / 15 minutes
- **Route:** A one-way descent into the north end of the canyon. The trail passes between two rock walls at The Chute, beginning with an immediate 22-degree descent, then makes several sharp turns toward the canyon floor and follows a creek bed to Tin Tin.
- **Difficulty and caution:** Moderate, with sharp turns and steep descents but no significant obstacles. The new trail has soft dirt and soft edges; stay centered on the route while descending.
- **Getting there:** Starts on the east side of the Festival Hilltop camping area near the park entrance; the guide describes it as the fastest access to the canyon floor from the front gate.
- **Side trips:** Connects with Tin Tin near the entrance to Kate the Great.
- **Notable:** The rock-wall chute is a photo stop; the guide also recommends it for sunrise pictures.

### Landry's Landing

- **Rating:** Green 3 (Beginner)
- **Length and typical time:** 1 mile / 15 minutes
- **Route:** This canyon-access trail is named for Clint Landry, who designed and built many MERUS trails. It begins on Camp Point, descends into the canyon near MERUS 101 and the Drop In ridge, and ends at an old cattle gate connecting to the bottom of the Drop In and MERUS 101. The guide notes that, while under construction, it is open for one-way travel down into the canyon.
- **Difficulty and caution:** An easy descent with drops up to 17 degrees and a few sharp turns, but no serious obstacles. Stay centered because trail edges are soft; brush may grow close to the creek-bed section at the bottom.
- **Getting there:** Begin at the Area 170 campsite on the south side of Camp Point Trail.
- **Side trips:** None.
- **Notable:** The landing just after the first descent offers a notable canyon view.

### Sidewinder

- **Rating:** Blue 5 (Intermediate)
- **Length and typical time:** 1.54 miles / 35 minutes
- **Route:** Runs along the canyon rim and offers canyon views. After turning off Park Road West, the trail starts with a mogul, then winds through mesquite brush with tight turns, dips, and built-up hills. Near the merge onto MERUS 101, it has a steep descent followed by a sharp turn into an incline with a two-foot wall on one side. The guide says it can also be run in reverse, starting from MERUS 101.
- **Difficulty and caution:** Tight turns and short moguls challenge vehicle suspension; longer-wheelbase vehicles may need occasional three-point turns. The final descent and two-foot rock wall require careful assessment of vehicle and driver capability.
- **Getting there:** Take Park Road West from the MERUS office and turn left onto Sidewinder just past the V-notch.
- **Side trips:** None.
- **Notable:** Canyon views.
